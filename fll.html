What is FIRST LEGO League Challenge?

FIRST LEGO League Challenge is the entry point into competition robotics for elementary and middle school students. Teams build and program a LEGO SPIKE robot to complete missions on a themed game table, research a real-world problem and pitch a solution, and grow as a team through FIRST's Core Values. It's serious engineering made age-appropriate — and a natural launchpad toward FTC.

Who it's for

FLL Challenge is built for students in grades 4–8. No experience needed — students start with the basics of building and block-based coding and grow into real robot design and strategy across the season.

What your student will do

  • Build and modify a LEGO SPIKE Prime robot with motors and sensors
  • Program autonomous missions using block-based and Python coding
  • Research a real-world problem and present an Innovation Project
  • Work as a team and practice FIRST Core Values — gracious professionalism
  • Compete at qualifying tournaments and present to judges

The season

Our FLL Challenge season runs through the fall — roughly two hours a week — building toward qualifying tournaments in November and December. The regional season culminates in the Virginia + DC Championship, hosted at James Madison University in January.

The path to the Championship

Qualifying Tournament (Nov–Dec)→ VA-DC Championship · JMU (January)

Teams advance through Virginia + DC FIRST LEGO League qualifying tournaments to the regional championship at James Madison University. Standout teams may earn invitations to the FIRST LEGO League World Festival.

Why FLL matters

FLL Challenge builds the foundation: real coding and engineering habits, teamwork, and the confidence to present ideas out loud — all before high school. It's also the direct feeder into FTC, so students who start here are ready to step up to the next level when they're older.
