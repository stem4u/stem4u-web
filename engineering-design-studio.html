Learn it, then engineer it

Great engineering starts with understanding how things work and figuring out how to make them better. At Engineering Design Studio, students learn to design, develop, and fabricate real-world solutions. Starting with the fundamentals of Onshape 3D CAD, students progress into mechanical components, multi-part assemblies, and functional engineering systems.

They then bring their designs into the physical world. Students explore how different manufacturing processes work and how to choose the right process for a particular part or project. Through 3D printing, laser cutting, and CNC machining demonstrations and supervised fabrication activities, they discover how digital designs become physical products.

Rather than simply following CAD tutorials, students take on engineering challenges, develop their own solutions, build prototypes, evaluate performance, and refine their designs. Learn the tools. Understand the engineering. Make your ideas real.

The STEM4U Workflow

Design-to-Fabrication

From an idea to a physical prototype, students experience the full engineering process.

1
Define
Understand the problem, requirements, and design constraints.
2
Design
Develop concepts and create 3D CAD models in Onshape.
3
Engineer
Refine components, dimensions, assemblies, and mechanisms.
4
Fabricate
Create parts with 3D printing, laser cutting, or CNC machining.
5
Test
Assemble the prototype and evaluate how it performs.
6
Improve
Identify issues, revise the design, and build a better version.

What students will explore

A progression from CAD fundamentals to engineering design and fabrication.

CAD Foundations & Product Design

Learn professional 3D CAD using Onshape, from the fundamentals to more advanced modeling.

  • Sketching, dimensions, and constraints
  • 3D modeling and parametric design
  • Design features, patterns, and complex geometries
  • Create functional mechanical components

Mechanical Design & Assemblies

Understand how individual components work together to form mechanical systems.

  • Gears, shafts, brackets, and mechanical components
  • Multi-part modeling and assemblies
  • Assembly relationships and motion
  • Design functional mechanisms and systems

Fabrication & Manufacturing

Explore how engineering designs are transformed into physical products.

  • 3D printing and additive manufacturing
  • Laser cutting for precision parts, panels, and enclosures
  • Introduction to CNC machining and subtractive manufacturing
  • Materials, tolerances, clearances, and fit
  • Design for manufacturing (DFM)
  • How to select an appropriate fabrication method

Prototyping, Testing & Iteration

Apply engineering principles to build, evaluate, and improve designs.

  • Translate CAD models into physical prototypes
  • Assemble and test components
  • Identify design limitations and areas for improvement
  • Refine designs based on testing and feedback

Engineering Projects & Capstone

Put engineering skills into practice through larger design challenges.

  • Understand project requirements and constraints
  • Brainstorm and develop design solutions
  • Create CAD models and mechanical assemblies
  • Fabricate and test prototypes
  • Evaluate performance and improve designs
  • Document and present engineering projects

Tools, technologies & fabrication resources

An engineering design and fabrication environment where students move from digital concepts to physical creations.

  • Onshape 3D CAD — professional cloud-based modeling, parametric design, components, and assemblies
  • 3D printing — additive manufacturing for rapid prototyping and functional components
  • Laser cutting — precision cutting of sheet materials for panels, enclosures, and structural parts
  • CNC machining — introduction to subtractive manufacturing and design for manufacturing
  • Mechanical design & assembly — tools and components for mechanisms, motion, fit, and assemblies
  • Engineering measurement & testing — tools for evaluating dimensions, fit, functionality, and performance
A note on equipment access: some equipment and processes are instructor-demonstrated or used only under direct supervision. We confirm which machines students operate themselves before each project, with safety first.

By the end, students will be able to

  • Create parametric 3D models using professional CAD tools
  • Design mechanical components using dimensions, constraints, and engineering principles
  • Develop multi-part assemblies and understand how mechanisms work
  • Select appropriate fabrication methods for different design requirements
  • Understand the fundamentals of 3D printing, laser cutting, and CNC machining
  • Apply design-for-manufacturing principles
  • Build, test, evaluate, and improve physical prototypes
  • Document and present engineering projects

Where this leads

Engineering Design Studio helps students develop a foundation in mechanical engineering, product design, prototyping, and manufacturing. The skills built here support students interested in:

  • Mechanical and manufacturing engineering
  • Robotics and automation
  • Product design and development
  • Aerospace and automotive engineering
  • Advanced STEM programs and engineering competitions
  • College engineering and technology pathways

Students interested in robotics can also apply these skills to FTC and other advanced robotics projects.
