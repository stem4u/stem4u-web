Pollinator Alley

Keeping pollinators alive and safe

Driving question: How do we keep pollinators alive and safe?
Do it live: hold up a flower picture and a mowed-grass picture — which has more bees? 🐝
Why it matters

Pollinators feed us

  • Bees carry pollen so plants make fruit and seeds
  • About one third of what farms grow needs pollinators
  • More than 3 of 4 food-crop types need them at least a little
Say one fact you checked (USDA / IPBES).
The problem

They're in trouble

  • People cut down flowers and clear land
  • Pesticides can poison pollinators
  • A changing climate makes it harder
Our solution

Pollinator Alley

Big garden

Native flowers to live and eat

No pesticides

Keep poison out

Covered roads

Keep bees and cars apart

AI cameras

Count pollinators, send data to scientists

Where it could go

Data Center Alley

Loudoun County has ~200 data centers surrounded by mowed grass and power lines. Turn those green strips into one long flower path — meadows between the buildings, maybe gardens on low roofs.

This is our big idea — we still need to check the data-center facts.
What we found

Our count test

  • Watch a flower patch and a mowed-grass patch for 10 minutes each
  • Tally every pollinator that lands — on 3 different days
  • We expect the flower patch wins by a lot
Show the tally chart.
Our recommendations

What we'd tell a community

  • Turn mowed strips & power-line land into native flower meadows
  • No pesticides on the meadows
  • Add a covered crossing where the path meets a road
  • Use AI cameras and share the data with scientists
